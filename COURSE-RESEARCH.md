# Course-design review — 18 September 2026

Codex read the sources below during the final alignment review, after the
initial course and Policy Lab already existed. This is not evidence that the
author read them earlier. The interpretations and proposed applications below
remain available for the author's review alongside PROCESS.md.

## Sources and decisions

1. [Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center: alignment of assessments, objectives and
   instruction](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/alignment.html).
   The guidance connects intended capabilities, assessment evidence and
   opportunities to practise. Applied here, a shared topic is insufficient:
   the action rehearsed must match the action assessed. This review exposed
   Week 3's switch between omitting a recorded wait and changing a service
   duration. The revision teaches both explicitly but maps only the first to
   Queue Autopsy. This is a review lens, not empirical proof this course works.
2. [Calling Bullshit: syllabus](https://callingbullshit.org/syllabus.html),
   Carl Bergstrom and Jevin West. Its stated outcomes progress from noticing
   questionable claims to explaining their defects to different audiences;
   its modules sustain that concern across a semester. The useful comparison
   is whether a striking title leads to a demonstrable capability. SLOP2516
   retains its narrower institutional case and restrained voice; it does not
   import that syllabus, reading list or confrontational register. Its endpoint
   remains a written redesign that can explain who bears a policy's cost.
3. [Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center: inclusive
   assessments](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/classroomclimate/strategies/designassessment.html).
   The guidance recommends practice, clear expectations and appropriate
   flexibility in how learning is demonstrated. Here, analysis must not depend
   on witnessing a dramatic outlier or being able to observe in person. The
   revision accepts an honestly ordinary longest wait and explicitly honours
   approved observation records. Written objections can replace oral rehearsal;
   the final assessment remains written. No claim is made to have read the
   book cited by this guidance or to have tested these changes with learners.
