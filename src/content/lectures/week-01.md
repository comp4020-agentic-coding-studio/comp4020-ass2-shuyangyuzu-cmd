Every week of this course adds one artefact to the same file: the SlopU
Office of Everything, a single counter that queues everything the
university's central services touch. This lecture sets up why a fictional
office is useful at all, and draws the line the course keeps returning to —
between "a delay happened" and "a waiting rule was designed."

## Outline

- the course claim: waiting allocates service, risk, attention and priority —
  it is not simply the absence of service
- the Office of Everything: one counter, one ticket system, everything from
  enrolment variations to lost property routed through it
- "a delay happened" (something went wrong) versus "a waiting rule was
  designed" (someone chose an ordering, on purpose, with consequences)
- what counts as evidence in this course: the Office of Everything is
  fiction, built for the exercises; claims about real queueing systems are
  kept separate and kept plain
- the shape of the semester — measure, choose, communicate, defend — and
  where week 1's observation exercise fits into it

> **Office of Everything file — synthetic case material.** The counter opened
> this semester with a single rule printed above the ticket dispenser: "Take
> a number. Numbers are served in order." No other policy is recorded yet —
> that absence is itself a decision, and next week's session starts by
> noticing what it doesn't say.

The Queue Lab this week asks you to watch a real queue — not the Office of
Everything's — closely enough to notice where the "delay happened / rule was
designed" line actually falls in something you can see.
