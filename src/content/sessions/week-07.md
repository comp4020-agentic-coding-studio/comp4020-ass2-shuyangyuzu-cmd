This lab does not build an estimator. It asks what an honest sign could say
using only the facts a real front counter already has to hand.

## Before the lab

Bring your chosen policy and defence from week 6; the sign you design this
week has to be honest about whichever policy the Office is actually running.

## In the lab

> **Office of Everything file — synthetic case material.** The counter can
> know, at any moment: how many tickets are currently waiting, which policy
> is in effect (from week 6), and whether the case currently being served
> has already run longer than a typical case for its category.

List what those three facts do and don't tell you about how long a new
arrival will wait. Then draft the sign's actual wording for two situations
using your week-6 policy: a short queue (two tickets waiting, nothing
unusual in progress) and a long one (twelve tickets waiting, the case in
progress already overrunning). Your wording does not need a number if a
number would be dishonest — a stated range, a plain acknowledgement of
uncertainty, or a comparison ("longer than usual today") are all valid, as
long as they are true given what the counter knows.

Write one sentence naming what your wording deliberately refuses to promise
(an exact minute count, a guarantee against further delay) and why promising
it would be dishonest rather than merely optimistic.

## Afterwards

Keep this sign design; it is part of what the Policy Trial, due this week,
asks you to include when you defend your chosen policy on a declared
dataset. Week 8 asks a related but sharper question: not what a queue can
promise while it's moving, but how anyone can tell when it's stopped moving
altogether.
