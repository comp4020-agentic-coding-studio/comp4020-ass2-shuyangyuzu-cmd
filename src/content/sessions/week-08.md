The Thursday incident looked stuck to everyone in line and wasn't. This lab
asks what evidence would have told them otherwise, and how long they should
reasonably have waited for it before leaving.

## Before the lab

Reread the incident report from this week's lecture carefully — note
exactly what information reached the waiting crowd, and what reached only
staff.

## In the lab

List everything a person standing in that Thursday queue could actually
observe: the display board's number, whether the physical line was moving,
whether anyone was visibly served, whether staff were present and attending
to anyone. Mark which of these actually changed during the forty-minute jam
and which stayed constant in a way that looked like evidence of a stall.

Write a specific rule: after how many minutes of no visible change should
someone in that queue reasonably conclude it is stuck rather than merely
slow? Defend your number using only the evidence you listed — not a general
intuition about patience.

Then apply the same question to the real system you have been tracking.
Name one specific, observable signal that would tell you it had stopped
rather than slowed, and say whether that signal is currently visible to
someone waiting in it, or only to staff.

## Afterwards

Keep your stuck/slow rule. Week 9 turns to a related failure: not a queue
that stops, but one where people leave it before being served, and what
that departure costs a system that measures itself only by the people who
stay.
