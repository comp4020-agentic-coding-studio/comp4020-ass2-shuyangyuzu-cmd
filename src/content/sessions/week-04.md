The complaint from this week's lecture is easy to agree with and hard to act
on. This lab asks you to turn "that's not fair" into a specific proposed
rule, and then to price it.

## Before the lab

Reread the students' association complaint. Notice what it does and doesn't
specify: it objects to the outcome, but it doesn't say what ordering rule
should replace FCFS.

## In the lab

> **Office of Everything file — synthetic case material.** This week's
> ticket log again: nine short "collect a form" requests (2 minutes each)
> and one "dispute a printer credit" request (20 minutes), arriving in the
> order 1 (dispute), 2 through 10 (forms).

Under the Office's current FCFS rule, tickets 2 through 10 each wait behind
the 20-minute dispute. Propose a specific alternative ordering for this exact
log — not a general principle, an ordering you could hand to the person
running the counter. State it as a rule someone else could follow without
asking you what to do next.

Now check your rule against the case it makes worse: if you moved the short
requests ahead of the dispute, work out how much longer ticket 1 now waits,
and write one paragraph arguing whether that trade is justified for the
Office of Everything specifically — not queues in general.

## Afterwards

Keep your proposed rule. Weeks 5 and 6 will each give you a different
alternative to FCFS to test against this same log, and week 6's Queue Lab
compares all of them side by side ahead of the Policy Trial.
