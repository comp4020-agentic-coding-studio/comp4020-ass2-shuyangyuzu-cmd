The first Queue Lab has no simulator, no dataset and no spreadsheet. It has
one job: pick a real waiting system you can watch again over the next two
weeks, and watch it once, carefully, before you have any theory about it.

## Before the lab

Pick a system you can plausibly observe three times across the next three
weeks — the same café queue, the same pharmacy counter, the same help desk.
It needs to be public to watch (see the policies page on observing public
systems without collecting anyone's personal data) and it needs to actually
have a queue, not just a single server with occasional customers.

## In the lab

Watch your chosen system for at least fifteen minutes. Write down two
separate columns: what you observed (a person arrived, a person left a
counter, a person walked away) and what you assumed (that arrival order is
service order, that the person who left the counter was "done," that the
person who walked away gave up rather than finished). Most first attempts at
this collapse the two columns into one without noticing.

Then answer the lecture's question for your system specifically: does the
wait begin when someone joins a physical line, when they take a ticket, when
they notice the queue exists, or somewhere else? Different systems will
answer this differently, and the answer changes what "wait time" would even
mean if you tried to measure it.

## Afterwards

Keep your notes. Week 2 asks you to start counting; week 3 asks you to
explain an outlier. All three feed the Queue Autopsy, due at the end of week
3, which is this same system, written up properly.
