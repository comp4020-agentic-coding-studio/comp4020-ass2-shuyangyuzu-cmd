This is the last Queue Lab, and it has one task: turn twelve weeks of notes
into a redesign you could actually defend out loud.

## Before the lab

Gather every decision you have made about the Office of Everything so far:
your week 6 policy choice and its defence, your week 7 sign wording, your
week 8 stuck/slow rule, your week 9 reneging finding, your week 10 audit
conclusion, and your week 11 traced failure point.

## In the lab

Write your redesign as a short, specific document, not a narrative: state
the ordering policy the counter will run (keep, modify or replace your
week 6 choice, and say which), the wording rule for what it tells someone
waiting (building on week 7), the specific signal that distinguishes a stall
from a slow patch (from week 8), and what the counter will now do
differently to account for people who leave before being served (from
week 9). Address the Priority Lane directly, using your week 10 audit as
evidence either for keeping it, changing its verification, or removing it.

For each of those four decisions, name the alternative you rejected and the
one sentence you would give if asked to justify rejecting it. Then pick the
single decision you think is weakest under questioning — the one a sceptical
reader would push hardest on — and write a short defence of it that also
states plainly what you would concede if the pushback turned out to be
right. A defence that concedes nothing is not a defence, it's a restatement.

## Afterwards

This redesign and its defence are the direct basis for The Honest Queue,
due this week, which asks for the same material written up formally and
verified against the requirements it sets out. This is the last Queue Lab of
the semester.
