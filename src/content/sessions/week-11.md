The worst day had several things wrong with it at once. This lab asks you to
separate the ones that were bad luck from the one that was a choice — and to
argue for changing the choice.

## Before the lab

Reread the incident summary from this week's lecture, and have your week 6
policy choice, week 8 stuck/slow rule, and week 10 Priority Lane audit ready
to reference.

## In the lab

List the incident's contributing factors separately: the volume surge (an
external event, not a design choice), the dispenser jam (a recurring failure
mode identified back in week 8), and the disproportionate rise in declared
urgency under pressure (a use of the Priority Lane your week 10 audit should
already have flagged as plausible).

Argue for one specific decision, from any earlier week, that you would
change first if you could only change one thing before this happened again.
It does not have to be the Priority Lane — it could be the choice not to
track issued-versus-served tickets from week 9, the choice of policy from
week 6, or the decision not to give the dispenser a fallback from week 8.
Defend your choice by naming what, specifically, would have gone differently
on this exact worst day if it had been made differently.

Then write one honest sentence about the cost: what would your proposed
change have cost the Office on an ordinary day, when nothing was going
wrong? A change that only pays off during a crisis and does nothing but add
overhead the rest of the time is a real trade-off, not a free improvement.

## Afterwards

You now have everything the final redesign needs to draw on: a measurement
method, a compared and audited set of policies, a standard for honest
communication, a stuck/slow distinction, and one identified point of
failure. Week 12 asks you to redesign the Office of Everything properly and
defend it under questioning.
