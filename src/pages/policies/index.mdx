# Policies and support

These rules apply across all three assessments and every Queue Lab. Where an
assessment task needs one of them, it links here rather than restating it.

## Accessibility of the course

Lecture pages and Queue Lab pages are plain text with structured headings, so
they work with screen readers and translation tools without modification.
Slide decks (see the week 1 deck) carry the same content as their lecture
page in prose form, so nothing in the course depends on being able to see a
slide. If a Queue Lab activity assumes a sense or a physical action you
cannot use — the observation exercises in weeks 1 through 3 in particular ask
you to watch a system in person — contact the convenor before the relevant
week to arrange an alternative source of observation data, such as a written
account from someone else or a recorded log. This is a standing offer, not a
special favour, because the assessments care about the analysis, not about
how the observation was physically made.

## Observing public systems without collecting personal data

The Queue Autopsy and its supporting Queue Labs (weeks 1 through 3) ask you
to watch a real, public waiting system. You may record timing, counts, and
what you observe about the system's behaviour. You may not record anything
that identifies a specific person — no names, no photographs, no
recognisable descriptions of individuals, and no recording of what anyone
says. If a system cannot be observed this way (because, for instance,
distinguishing individual cases requires identifying who they are), pick a
different system rather than working around this rule.

## Synthetic data only, for anything about the Office of Everything

Every dataset, memo, incident report and complaint attributed to the SlopU
Office of Everything in this course is invented for teaching purposes. When
an assessment or Queue Lab asks you to build on that material — extending a
ticket log, constructing a comparable dataset for the Policy Trial — your
addition must be clearly constructed data, declared as such, not data
presented as if it were observed. Do not attribute invented Office of
Everything material to any real organisation, and do not import real
queueing datasets into the fiction; the two are kept separate throughout
this course, and mixing them undermines both.

## Respectful critique

Several Queue Labs and both the Policy Trial and The Honest Queue ask you to
argue that one design choice is better than another, and to say who a
choice disadvantages. Direct that critique at the design, not at whoever
might have made it — in your own work, in a classmate's work if it comes up
in discussion, or in the fictional Office of Everything's decisions. "This
policy starves long requests under sustained short-request arrivals" is the
kind of claim this course wants; claims about the competence or character of
whoever chose a policy are not, and add nothing a marker can check.

## Extensions and late work

If you need more time on an assessment, ask the convenor before the deadline
where possible, or as soon as you reasonably can afterwards. State what you
need and why; a short, specific request is easier to grant quickly than a
general one. This page describes how extensions work for SLOP2516
specifically — it is not a statement of university-wide policy, and nothing
here should be read as describing rules that apply outside this course.

## Using and citing sources

You may use verified external sources where they are genuinely relevant —
to interpret an observation, check a definition, or understand a method —
and where you do, cite them. This course does not require you to have read
academic literature on queueing, and does not reward pretending to have.

Two limits apply to how a source can be used. An external source cannot
substitute for direct evidence about a real queue you personally observed:
if a Queue Lab or the Queue Autopsy asks what happened in the system you
watched, that answer has to come from your observation, not from a source
describing queues in general. And anything drawn from the SlopU Office of
Everything is synthetic case material, not real-world evidence — cite or
identify it as course material when you build on it, and never present it
as data about a real organisation.

If you make a specific, contestable claim about the real world in your own
writing, it needs a source. An ordinary calculation from data this course
has declared — a mean, a percentage, a comparison — doesn't need one; show
your working instead.

## Accessibility of what you submit

Where an assessment or Queue Lab asks for a written document, plain
structured text (headings, ordered steps, a table where a table is the
clearest way to show numbers) is sufficient and preferred over a heavily
designed layout. If you build any interactive artefact for The Honest Queue,
it must be operable by keyboard alone and must not rely on colour as the
only way of distinguishing information — the same standard this site itself
is built to.
